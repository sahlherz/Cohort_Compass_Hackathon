First, I want to discuss RateMyProfessor.

Personally, I go onto it to learn about what I can expect when taking a class. I look up the teacher for that class, see some things about hard/not hard, or teacher is nice/not nice, and get some rough data.

I mean, the reason why you would want to know about what a teacher is like is to help in your class-registration process.

But, I find RateMyProfessor to be quite lacking.
First, I have used RateMyProfessor many times and still always end up taking a class and having my picture of what the class is like either shattered, or, if I had no initial picture, only fully develop after like half a semester.

I had no idea what Discrete Structures taught until I took it: The course registration information is so wordy yet brief, using terms I can't understand until after the class itself, and with no reference to how in depth it goes on each thing or of their relative importances.
I had no idea what the *structure* of Discrete Structures was until I took it:
	Assignments started off being 15 hours per week and then became more like 10h/week, for the last bit of the course they were like 3h/week
	We also had one discussion assignment per week to be done with a group, it wasn't too long and was pretty manageable
	We went over logic and then basic proofs, especially by induction or contradiction, and we also went over some topics like Big O notation, probability stuff like counting and combinatorics, for example {possibly insert example question}, and blah blah X, the hardest part for me was Y.
	The skills in it ended up being useful for future CS classes but haven't been useful yet for any personal projects {people could click on the reviewer's profile and see some description of what they've done, possibly including projects} or for any real-world work.
	On the other hand, Data Structures, which I took at the same time, ended up being really useful for future programming stuff like understanding a core aspect of leetcode-type problems, or just building good programs really. It was recommended to take Data together with or after Discrete but I didn't find much overlap except for Big O

So, we could fill the need that RateMyProfessor fails to fulfill.

Second, we need our idea to be something people can use lifelong, even after they graduate.

They said they wanted something people can use in undergrad, grad school, and that follows them afterwards in their career. The last part may not be as feasible and thus not as important.

So, rather than only focusing on classes, we recognized that the same fundamental idea applies to all opportunities:
	Events, businesses and jobs in the area, businesses with UIowa grads in them, internships, random nice volunteering opportunities, study abroad stuff, extremely successful people (most CS majors at UoI do not know about Cesare Tinelli or how accomplished he is in his field), competitions, clubs
	You usually don't understand the reality of taking an opportunity until in the midst of doing so, unless you were told a lot about it by a friend. One club might really be a waste of time or only of very marginal benefit, while another club may completely upgrade your skillset. A research event may be prestigious but without opportunities for an undergraduate to discuss things much with researchers. One lab may make you do busywork while another gives you awesome stuff to work on. Or maybe it's important to know that a specific lab starts off with busywork but transitions to giving you awesome stuff.

So, instead of just asking people about classes, we want to ask people to give input about:
	critical factors for their successes 
	missed opportunities
	things we haven't already gotten much input about

and we want to do so in a way where the person doesn't feel annoyed giving a ton of info

my initial idea for this is that we have an ai ask them (in text; no need to have it speak aloud in my opinion... but maybe we can have an option for that. just depends on setup difficulty) questions based on the factors we saw earlier. maybe for this project we should finetune that ai?

and then, the person can answer OUT LOUD, and their spoken answer gets pieced apart and sorted into whatever categories. the ai can then either ask more questions or accept their answer. speaking out loud is faster/more convenient than typing most of the time, and helps preserve the "colloquial" type of response that we'd like to see, as this is part of what makes RateMyProfessor fun, and, crucially, it's often easier to give a lot of useful info fast in colloquial speech than in formal-ish writing

maybe we could have a separate option where they see some example great responses (similar to the discrete structures thing I explained above), optionally get asked a broad question (like, "tell me about X event"), and give a long free-response type of answer, then the ai can still probe them more

Essentially, I'd like it to be convenient for a person to give information. At the same time, we would like to get varied responses, a lot out of a person, and not annoy a person (so they should be able to stop giving info at any time, and we'd just use whatever they gave us).
Part of that convenience is giving them multiple choices for how they'd like to engage with it. Whether they'd like the AI to ask them about the thing, whether they'd like to just give a rant, whether they'd like a structured template to follow...

But in the end, what we want is useful information that isn't just some vague course listing like we see in MyUI, or some vague event description. we want info about how impactful it was for someone. about networking opportunities. about difficulty. about so many different things. and we'll have to get these in aggregate from a bunch of different people.




Ok I now understand how I want the part of someone looking at a specific event/course/whatever to look. I don't want it to be that there's a bunch of like sub-topics under pertaining to like, difficulty, networking, etc., and then the AI took pieces of people's responses and sorted it into those.
Instead, I want it to just be that if someone spoke about a certain class or event or whatever, their profile will show up in a list under that item when someone goes on the page for that item, and then under their profile there will be a sequence of the questions AI asked them (if any), and the responses to those questions. It can be like AI on the left, human on the right, like a messaging app.
Clicking on a person's profile takes you to their page. On their page is their responses, stuff like that, and under the profile icon thing is a small button or something saying Resume. Clicking on that just opens the Resume file in a new tab. That's just a resume that the person uploaded.
